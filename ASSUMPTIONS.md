# Assumptions & Limitations

Written so a reader can tell exactly which parts of this project are real
engineering and which parts are a model of reality.

## The headline caveat

**Every number in this repository comes from simulated data.** No production
records were used. The pipeline, the statistics, the tests, and the dashboard are
real and would run unchanged against a production database; the *inputs* are
generated by `src/simulate/generate_daily_sip_data.py`.

That means: **the method is the deliverable, not the findings.** "Day 5 is the
inflection point" is a demonstration that this pipeline can locate an inflection
point and quantify it, not a fact about any real user base. Against real data
the answer might be day 3 or day 9, and the code would find it.

## What is real

- The PostgreSQL schema, indexes, and views
- The gaps-and-islands streak construction, validated two independent ways
- Kaplan-Meier, Cox proportional hazards, log-rank, two-proportion z-test,
  chi-square, and Holm-Bonferroni correction, standard implementations,
  correctly applied to correctly-shaped inputs
- Censoring handling throughout
- The data quality checks and their proposed fixes
- The report automation, its alert thresholds, and its exit codes
- The Metabase dashboard and its provisioning
- 101 tests, CI on every pull request

## What is modelled

### User behaviour
Each user is a two-state machine. While active, they invest with a per-user
probability modulated by day-of-week and payday seasonality plus a mild
habit-strength term. While lapsed, their probability of returning decays
exponentially in the length of the gap.

**This is the key assumption, and it is doing a lot of work.** The exponential
decay in gap length is *why* recovery falls off between day 3 and day 7. Real
lapse behaviour might decay linearly, in steps, or with a threshold, and the
shape of that decay determines the intervention day. The analysis would correctly
recover a different shape; it just wasn't given one.

### Archetypes
Four behavioural profiles (`sticky_former`, `early_dropper`, `weekday_only`,
`payday_spiker`) with hand-chosen parameter ranges. Real populations are not
four clean clusters. The archetype labels live in `sim_user_profile`, a table
with no production equivalent, and are deliberately **excluded from the Cox
model**, predicting churn from the parameter churn was generated from would
measure the simulator, not users.

### The nudge effect
Modelled as a fixed multiplier (2.35×) on return probability, lasting 3 days.
Three limitations follow directly:

1. **No notification fatigue.** A user's tenth nudge is as effective as their
   first. Real fatigue would compress the day-5 advantage, and nothing here
   detects it.
2. **No channel differences.** Push, SMS, and email are assigned but have
   identical effects.
3. **No negative effects.** Real over-notification causes uninstalls. The model
   has no mechanism for a nudge to make things worse, so the analysis cannot
   find a harm that a real experiment might.

### Seasonality
Day-of-week multipliers and a payday cycle are hand-set to plausible values for
Indian retail fintech (weekend softness, a first-week-of-month spike). They're
informed guesses, not fitted to anything.

### Amounts
Drawn from {₹21, ₹51, ₹101} with fixed weights. Users don't change their
contribution size over time, which real investors do.

## Analytical choices worth arguing with

**Recovery horizon of 30 days.** A user returning on day 31 counts as churned.
Chosen to match the cohort D30 convention; a different horizon shifts absolute
rates, though the *ordering* of trigger days is stable across horizons.

**Failed and skipped transactions both break a streak.** Defensible, neither is
evidence the habit held. But a failed autopay mandate is the product's fault
and a skip is the user's choice. Treating them identically means a payment outage
would register as mass habit collapse. Worth splitting in production.

**"Retained" means transacting.** Deliberately stricter than the industry
convention. See `src/analysis/cohort_analysis.py` for all three definitions
reported side by side.

**Cox clustered on `user_id`.** 50,576 streaks come from 5,000 users. Without
clustering, standard errors shrink by roughly the square root of that ratio and
several covariates look significant when they aren't, as they did before the
clustering was added.

## Data quality issues are deliberate

The defects found by `src/analysis/data_quality.py` were **seeded on purpose** by
the generator (`SEED_RATES`), and the schema deliberately omits the constraints
that would prevent them. This is so the detection and remediation work is
demonstrable rather than hypothetical. See `data_quality_findings.md`.

One exception: **DQ-05** (450 users transacting without completed KYC) was *not*
seeded directly. It emerged from KYC status and transaction behaviour being drawn
independently, which is exactly how the equivalent bug arises in production, and
a fair illustration of why the check is worth having.

## Reproducibility

Fixed seed (`SIM_RANDOM_SEED=42`) over `2025-01-01` to `2025-12-31`. Same seed,
same 373,387 rows, byte for byte, there is a test asserting it. Changing the
seed changes every number in `MEMO.md`; the qualitative conclusions hold, the
decimals do not.
